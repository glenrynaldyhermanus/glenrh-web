Chapter One

Me

The longer story: where I’ve been building, how I think, and what’s in my bag.

3

The short version

I build the product and the business around it.

My name is Glen. I’ve been shipping software since April 2011, and today I’m Technical Lead at Cinema XXI, Indonesia’s largest cinema chain. I started on mobile, and I still love it. But one skill isn’t enough anymore, so I build the whole thing: native apps, the Go services behind them, the POS at the counter, the dashboard for the owner and the AI agents that keep it all moving.

Lately that has meant a cinema app, a padel club, a hiring pipeline, a pet-industry platform, EV chargers and a short-drama studio. Most of it started with someone saying “this is annoying”.

The road so far

Fifteen years, four desks.

  1. 2011 to 2015

    Padicon

    My first job. I built Indosat Sakuku, a web terminal for patching Cisco routers, and Padiciti, an online travel agent for trains, flights and hotels. Padiciti was mine to champion: the design, the UX and the apps on BlackBerry, Android, iOS and Windows Mobile.

  2. 2015 to 2018

    Indivara and Jatis

    Prototypes for Mentimun (an online-to-offline shop for agents) and a mutual fund app, then Aladdin, a B2B travel app for agents. I also built the My JNE tracking app, which has since moved to another vendor.

  3. 2018 to 2022

    MoGawe, my own startup

    As CTO of PT Media Baru Internasional, I built a field-operations ecosystem: companies hired crowd workers to check their stores and sales teams in the field. Clients included Danone and Unilever.

  4. 2022 to now

    Cinema XXI

    I started on m.tix, the app people use to buy cinema tickets and snacks. Now I also lead the core systems, the outlet POS and Cineflow, our desktop copilot. Read about m.tix

  5. On the side

    Businesses I helped build

    Anterin (2016), Daihatsu’s owner manual and a voice-driven workshop tablet that Daihatsu Japan praised (2019), Allnimall (2022), Transmore (2024), Ourbit and Climaxx (2025), and Metropole Padel and ShtEV (2026). See them all

How I think

Opinions, loosely held.

  1. Start with the business.

    Who pays, who uses it and who runs it every day. The app is only one of those three.

  2. Native when it matters.

    If an app lives in someone’s pocket all day, it should feel like it belongs on that phone.

  3. Boring backends.

    Go, PostgreSQL and clear contracts. The excitement belongs in the product, not in the pager.

  4. Mock the world, then swap it.

    Payments, hardware and AI sit behind interfaces, so the screens can ship before every integration is ready.

  5. Parity is a feature.

    macOS and Windows, Android and iOS: the same behaviour and the same words on every platform.
